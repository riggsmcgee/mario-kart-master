← → or A/D to steer. Space to hop. G toggles smart steering. R to reset. There is no accelerator — the kart always drives forward, exactly like Jodi's auto-accelerate setting.

Jodi drives with smart steering on, so that is the default here. Drive a lap, press G, drive another: the difference is exactly what her setting does for her. If our version is stronger than the real game's she will learn to lean on help the Switch will not give her, so this one wants to be honest rather than generous.

Orange with chevrons is a boost pad. Purple ramps launch you — hop near the lip and you land with a bigger boost than any pad gives, and the kart barrel-rolls to tell you it counted. Note the pad on the outside of the big sweeping turn: sometimes the fast way round is not the tight way round, which is Chapter 4's whole point.

What to feel for

This is a rehearsal for gate 1b6, so the questions are the same. Does the view read as “Mario Kart-ish” instantly? Is the steering responsive without being twitchy — can you hold a smooth line through the corner, or does it snap? Does the back end stepping out feel like a kart or like ice? Does grass feel like a real penalty without feeling like a punishment? Does hitting the wall cost you something?

The camera is a feel system, not plumbing. Almost all of it is lag: a camera welded rigidly behind the kart looks correct and feels dead. Camera heading lag is the biggest single contributor to “that looks like Mario Kart” — it lets you see the kart's flank as it turns. Speed FOV is what makes fast feel fast. Try both at zero to hear the difference.

Every number below the canvas is live. When something feels wrong, move the slider until it does not, then hit Copy TUNING.md entry and tell me what you changed and why.
