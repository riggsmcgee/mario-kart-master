Shield Up

Step 1e1. The kart piece with something chasing it. Drive through an item box to pick up a banana; when the siren says a red shell has locked on from behind, hold the item key and the banana trails behind your bumper and takes the hit. Chapter 2's whole lesson is that holding it costs you nothing, so hold it.

Drive

← → or A/D to steer. Shift holds the banana behind you. R resets.

There is no magic shield here, just a banana. Hold Shift and it sits behind your bumper, where the shell hits it instead of you. Let go and it falls on the road behind you and it is gone.

A locked-on shell does not miss. No near misses, no veering off, no lucky escapes — if it is coming and the banana is not behind you when it arrives, it hits you. Anything softer would teach that not holding sometimes works out, which is the exact belief this drill exists to remove.

Bananas come from the item boxes, four rows of them spread around the lap. Blocking uses yours up, so after a good block the next job is finding another box.

Windows note: holding right Shift for eight seconds pops up the Filter Keys prompt. Use the left one, or rebind the item key on the input prototype. Worth knowing before we pick final bindings at 1g1.

Telemetry

Threats

    blocked held it all the way · dropped let go while it was coming · hit banana in hand, never held out · unarmed nothing to defend with.

    What to feel for

    Rehearsal for gate 1e2, which asks two things. Is the threat legible? There is deliberately no top-down view on this page: an instrument showing the shell closing in from above would answer the question for you. The siren, the draining bar and the pips are all you get, which is all Jodi will get.

    Is the hold-the-banana habit forming after two minutes? The honest test is whether you start holding before the siren rather than after it. If you are still reacting at the end of two minutes, the warning lead is probably too short — move it on the panel and say what felt right.

    Also worth a verdict: the sound. If the pips are annoying rather than useful, that is a real finding and not a nitpick — they are doing the job of reaching you while you are looking at the road, and if they fail at that they are just noise.

    The fake-out warnings from the original 1e1 wording are gone, following “that’s not a thing” — every siren now means a real shell that will really arrive. Say if you want some form of false alarm back; without them the only ways to be hit are having no banana, or having one and not holding it.
