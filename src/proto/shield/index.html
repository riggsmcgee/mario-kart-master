Shield Up

Step 1e1. The kart piece with something chasing it. A siren means a red shell has locked on from behind — hold the item key and whatever you are carrying takes the hit instead of you. Chapter 2's whole lesson is that holding it costs you nothing, so hold it.

Drive

← → or A/D to steer. Shift holds the shield up. R resets.

The green item behind your kart is your armour. Hold Shift and it stays back there covering you; let go and you throw it away, exactly as the real game does. That is the one thing this drill charges you for — holding early is always free.

Roughly a third of the warnings are fake: identical sirens, identical approach, and then the thing veers off in the last fraction of a second. They are not there to punish holding. They are there to bait the flinch — the relieved release that leaves you empty-handed with the next siren already starting.

Windows note: holding right Shift for eight seconds pops up the Filter Keys prompt. Use the left one, or rebind the item key on the input prototype. Worth knowing before we pick final bindings at 1g1.

    What to feel for

    Rehearsal for gate 1e2, which asks two things. Is the threat legible? There is deliberately no top-down view on this page: an instrument showing the shell closing in from above would answer the question for you. The siren, the draining bar and the pips are all you get, which is all Jodi will get.

    Is the hold-to-shield habit forming after two minutes? The honest test is whether you start holding before the siren rather than after it. If you are still reacting at the end of two minutes, the warning lead is probably too short — move it on the panel and say what felt right.

    Also worth a verdict: the fake-outs. Thirty per cent is a guess. Too many and the sirens stop meaning anything; too few and the relieved release never gets caught out. And the sound — if the pips are annoying rather than useful, that is a real finding, not a nitpick.
