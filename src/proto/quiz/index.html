Quiz card

Step 1d1. A situation, two to four big buttons, and a warm answer either way. Chapter 2 uses it for item decisions and Chapter 4 for spotting boost pads — both are the same shape of question: here is where everyone is, what do you do?

Click a button or press 1, 2, 3. After answering, Enter moves on — focus lands on the next button, so a run of cards never needs the mouse.

The picture is drawn from the same JSON that writes the question — a road, and a list of who is standing on it. That is what keeps Chapter 2's eight to twelve situations from turning into eight to twelve illustration jobs, and it means no art asset ever comes near this repo.

    What to feel for

    This is the rehearsal for gate 1d2, and the question there is does this feel like a fun quiz moment, not a test? Three things to watch. Does a wrong answer sting, or does it read as someone explaining something to you? Nothing here is red, there is no timer and no streak to protect — on purpose, but tell me if it still feels like being marked.

    Second: every option carries its own reply, so picking the wrong one tells you why that one loses rather than just naming the winner. Is that worth the extra writing? It is roughly three times the copy per card.

    Third, the pacing. One card, answer, one paragraph, next. Too fast? Too slow? And the copy is a first draft in the intended voice — if the jokes are wrong, or if Kayla would never do that, say so.
